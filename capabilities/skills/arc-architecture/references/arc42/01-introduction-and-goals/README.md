# 1. Introduction and Goals

**Purpose.** Frame the system through driving requirements, a small set of architecture quality goals, and stakeholder expectations.

## Subsections

- **1.1 Requirements Overview.** Use it when the project has relevant information; preserve project-specific naming or grouping.
- **1.2 Quality Goals.** Use it when the project has relevant information; preserve project-specific naming or grouping.
- **1.3 Stakeholders.** Use it when the project has relevant information; preserve project-specific naming or grouping.

## Evidence to examine

- Approved product goals, requirements, use cases, and canonical links.
- Measured or scenario-based quality goals and their stakeholders.
- Existing stakeholder maps or ownership records.

Use these as investigation leads, not automatic proof. Prefer current implementation, tests, configuration, and maintainer-confirmed decisions appropriate to the claim. Cite concrete repository evidence in resulting documentation. Separate observed behavior, accepted intent, inference, and proposal.

## Gaps and conflicts

- If evidence does not establish useful content, state what is missing and ask the maintainer for context.
- If sources disagree, describe the competing evidence and its source; do not silently choose one. Ask the maintainer to resolve intent or status.
- Do not invent names, responsibilities, boundaries, measures, owners, dates, or rationales to make the section appear complete.
- If the project omits, combines, or adapts this section, follow that choice and link to the content location when known.

## Practical guidance

- Keep the overview selective and link source requirements rather than duplicating them.
- Keep the top three to five quality goals here; put full requirements in Section 10.
- Use concrete scenarios and make assumptions explicit when goals are not known.
